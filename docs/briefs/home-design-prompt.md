# Prompt: Rapid Medical home page design (for another design agent)

Prepared 2026-11-27. Describes the direction, the brand and the content, and deliberately leaves the design decisions
to the agent. Paste everything below the line.

---

Design the home page of a new website for Rapid Medical, a neurovascular medtech company. Deliver one self-contained
HTML page that works on desktop and on a phone, plus a short note (5–8 sentences) explaining your visual idea.

## The company and the audience
Rapid Medical makes devices for treating stroke: a clot retriever for large vessel stroke (TIGERTRIEVER™), an assist
device for brain aneurysms (COMANECI™) and a steerable guidewire for access (DRIVEWIRE™). What sets them apart: the
physician can adjust the device in real time, inside the vessel, to fit the patient's anatomy. The main audience is
neurointerventional physicians; investors and partners read the site too. The tone is confident, precise and calm,
never consumer-style or salesy.

## Visual direction: "Generative Biotech"
The client chose this direction. Treat it as a mood and a set of principles, not as a layout to copy.
- A dark, focused, scientific field. Darker and more atmospheric than a typical medical site.
- One strong sculptural form per screen carries the emotional weight; everything around it stays quiet.
- Light comes from the object and from signals, as a controlled glow. No drop shadows.
- A particle / mesh / wireframe language: forms built from points and fine lines rather than glossy surfaces.
- Technical line art structures the page: hairlines, dot grids, measurement marks, coordinates, small monospaced labels.
- Interface chrome is restrained: panels are outlined rather than filled.
- Gradients only as a signal: the main action, one key word, the halo of an object.
- Motion is refined and data-inspired: slow depth shifts, light parallax, soft movement. Respect reduced motion.

What the client wants the page to feel like: **speed, fluidity, adjustability, premium devices, bold technology.**
One explicit request from the client: **avoid sharp angles and sharp elements.** Their trade-show booth is all arches,
circles and curves, and their brand imagery is flowing, glowing teal lines on dark backgrounds.

Avoid: light, airy, pastel or editorial looks (the client rejected that direction); frosted glass and soft shadows;
stock photos of smiling people; a generic corporate medtech template; purple and violet palettes (a direct competitor
uses them).

How you express the object, the motion, adjustability or the "no sharp angles" request is up to you.

## Brand (fixed)
Use these brand colors exactly. Gradients and dark backgrounds may be close rather than exact.
- Logo greens: #6CC24A (PMS 360, the core brand color), #009A44 (PMS 347), #046A38 (PMS 349)
- Secondary: #005776 (blue, PMS 7701), #279989 (cyan, PMS 7473)
- Grays: #333F48 (PMS 432, text), #97999B (Cool Gray 7)
- Brand gradient: from green #6CC24A through teal to blue #005776
- Product colors, only on product-specific elements: TIGERTRIEVER #FF6900, COMANECI #009A44, DRIVEWIRE #007398.
  Don't mix company and product styling in the same element.

Fonts (Google Fonts): Montserrat for headlines, Ubuntu for subheads and lead-ins, Lato for body text. A monospaced
companion for small labels is fine. The logo is the wordmark "RAPID MEDICAL" with a three-swoosh mark. Use it white on
dark backgrounds. If you don't have the file, set the wordmark in text.

## Imagery
The client has photorealistic device renders (many with transparent backgrounds), a looping animation of the DRIVEWIRE
handle bending the tip, fluoroscopy-style images, brand wallpapers with glowing teal flow lines, and simple rounded
line icons. If you can't access them, draw your own visuals in the spirit of the direction or use clearly labeled
placeholders. Don't use stock photography of people.

## Freedom and limits
Yours to decide: the hero concept and its object, the layout and rhythm of every section, how the story moves from
screen to screen, the motion and interactions, the imagery approach (generative, renders, abstract or a mix), and
how the dark field, glow and line art come together. Surprise us, but keep it believable for a medical device company
that physicians trust.

Limits: no invented clinical claims or numbers; text contrast that is readable on the dark field; works at phone
width without horizontal scrolling; motion stops under prefers-reduced-motion; everything stays in one HTML file.
